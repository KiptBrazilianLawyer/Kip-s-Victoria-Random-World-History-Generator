# Kip-s-Victoria-Random-World-History-Generator
A procedural world generator for Victoria 3 that creates complete alternate worlds from a seed, featuring new countries, borders, populations, cultures, economies, resources, trade, technology, diplomacy, and military forces while preserving geographic coherence, balance, and playability.

*(AI description)*

The Randomised World History Generator is a procedural alternate-world generator for Victoria 3. Using a seed and the options selected by the player, the program creates a new 1836 scenario with different countries, borders, populations, cultures, economies, natural resources, technology levels, diplomatic relationships, overseas territories, military forces, and trade networks. The project is not intended to generate a purely chaotic map. Its purpose is to create worlds that feel as though they emerged from a completely different historical development while preserving the economic, geographic, and political logic required for a functional campaign.

Every generated world can feature an entirely different international system. Historical powers may disappear, lose their importance, or remain relevant only when their territorial position makes sense, while historically secondary regions may become major population, industrial, commercial, or military centers. Procedural countries fill the map with their own names, colors, cultures, capitals, and borders. Depending on the selected settings, the world may consist of numerous small states, balanced compact countries, nations closer in size to those of the vanilla game, or a smaller number of large territorial blocs. Borders generally follow geographical continuity, reducing excessive enclaves, fragmentation, and unjustified disconnected territories.

The global balance of power is also generated procedurally. No region automatically receives the position it held in real history. A region becomes powerful when it receives a favorable combination of population, territory, resources, technology, infrastructure, industry, and access to trade. This allows unexpected regional and global powers to emerge, such as major industrial states in Africa, commercial centers in the Americas, alternative Asian empires, or naval powers in regions that normally have limited influence in 1836. Countries do not begin under identical conditions: some are wealthy and developed, others possess large populations but limited industry, while some face shortages, weak administration, or geographical disadvantages.

The starting economy is rebuilt according to the conditions of each seed. Countries receive rural, extractive, industrial, and administrative activities proportional to their population, available resources, territory, and development level. States may begin with food farms, livestock ranches, logging camps, fishing wharves, plantations, mines, and different industries. Smaller countries generally receive simpler economies focused on a limited number of sectors, while larger or more technologically advanced countries may begin with more diversified production chains. Starting production does not necessarily occupy the entire available potential, leaving room for industrialization, agricultural expansion, mining development, and future resource discoveries during the campaign.

Natural resources may be preserved, redistributed, or partially retained as discoverable deposits. Their distribution is intended to create regional specialization and economic dependency between countries. Some areas may concentrate iron and coal, while others rely on agriculture, timber, fishing, plantations, or rarer resources. Since countries are not expected to produce every good required by their economies, international trade becomes important from the beginning of the campaign. Shortages, surpluses, trade routes, and competition for strategic resources therefore develop differently in every world.

Markets begin with a functional commercial structure. Economically and politically eligible countries may possess Trade Centers, ports, convoys, and civilian shipyards. Their economies can import food, raw materials, and manufactured goods that are not produced domestically while exporting local surpluses. Coastal countries may develop stronger commercial integration and maritime capacity, while isolationist or landlocked nations may remain more dependent on domestic production. The availability of clippers, ports, and trade capacity allows the world market to operate from the beginning without removing the possibility of regional shortages and economic crises.

Population may remain relatively close to the original distribution or be reorganized according to the capacity of each state. Arable land, climate, natural resources, territorial size, regional density, and economic importance influence population concentration. Small islands and limited territories receive populations appropriate to their size, while large and fertile regions may become major demographic centers. Cultures may also be preserved or redistributed, creating new national formations, minorities, multicultural societies, internal tensions, and alternative patterns of migration and assimilation.

Technology and economic development can vary widely between countries and regions. The generated world may include advanced industrial centers, underdeveloped rural areas, states with strong administrative institutions, commercial economies, agrarian societies, or countries specializing in particular sectors. Essential technologies allow generated economies to operate at the beginning of the campaign, while advanced development remains uneven. Industrial, military, and scientific leadership may therefore emerge in regions that are completely different from those of the vanilla scenario.

Military forces are distributed according to the territorial and economic capacity of each country. Small or fragile nations may begin without a large standing army, while more populous and productive states can support barracks and larger land forces. Naval capability depends on coastline, ports, coastal population, technology, shipyards, available inputs, and fiscal resources. Some countries begin only with maritime trade infrastructure, others possess small civilian shipbuilding industries, and only genuinely capable nations begin with naval bases and military fleets. This allows land powers, naval powers, and hybrid powers to emerge without granting fixed advantages to historically dominant regions.

The generator may also create overseas territories, colonies, and subject states. Depending on the selected settings, the world may have no colonial empires, only a few coastal possessions, or a limited network of overseas domains and vassals. These territories are intended to form geographically coherent groups rather than scattered collections of unrelated possessions across several continents. Subjects are rare by default and tend to possess territorial proximity, a distinct cultural identity, and a plausible relationship with their overlord.

Strategic regions may also be reorganized to better match the territorial structure of the generated seed. Their boundaries and names reflect the geography of the alternate world, creating strategic areas that differ from those of the historical scenario. Names are localized according to the language used by the game, including Brazilian Portuguese, allowing procedural countries and regions to appear naturally within the Victoria 3 interface.

The generator provides different intensity levels and numerous options for controlling each part of the world. Players may preserve specific features of the original scenario while randomizing others, or rebuild almost the entire global situation of 1836. It can produce relatively familiar worlds, moderately alternate scenarios, or major political and economic transformations. Even identical settings can generate substantially different results when used with another seed.

Before the final mod is created, the program displays a preview of the generated world. This overview summarizes population distribution, resources, development, economic centers, favored regions, international balance, colonies, subjects, trade capacity, and military potential. The player can examine the scenario and decide whether to save it or generate another world.

The final result is an independent mod created specifically for that seed and ready to be enabled through the Victoria 3 launcher. Every generated world should be used with a new campaign. The generator does not permanently modify the original game installation and can be used to create as many different scenarios as the player wishes.

The Randomised World History Generator is designed for players who enjoy the economic, political, and social systems of Victoria 3 but want to explore a world in which the historical outcome has not already been determined. Every campaign begins with new powers, new markets, new rivalries, and new challenges, preserving the fundamental structure of the game while completely transforming the context in which it unfolds.
